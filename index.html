01 Pavilion study — north elevation

Fig. 01

From structure to atmosphere.

Every visualization begins as a set of decisions: a span, a joint, a degree of winter light. We keep that logic legible as the image acquires its materials.

Our studio works between the drawing and the film. We test how a building meets weather, how a surface receives light, and how a room feels when it is almost empty. The result is not a picture of architecture, but an argument for it.

02 Inside the design — level 02

Boundaries

Hover the markers to read the notes.

Inside the design.

A square plan, read from its outer boundary inward. The drawing assembles as you scroll — first the limits, then the structure that holds them.

  1. P.01

    Concept

    We begin with the argument of the building: its centre, its edges, and the path between them.

  2. P.02

    Material

    Timber, stone and glass are chosen for how they age in winter, not only for how they photograph.

  3. P.03

    Light

    Overcast light is patient. We place the warm interior against it so the windows read as inhabited.

  4. P.04

    Experience

    Finally the drawing becomes a place: water moving, snow settling, the camera allowed to wait.

04 Method — from parcel to place

Fig. 04

From parcel to place.

  1. R.01

    Receive

    You bring a model, a set of drawings, or a paragraph about the project. Rough is welcome — the argument matters more than the finish.

  2. R.02

    Distil

    We read the building: centre, edges, spans, the hour of light it wants. You get the reading back before anything is drawn.

  3. R.03

    Stage

    Camera, weather and material are choreographed — the same care the five views of this pavilion received.

  4. R.04

    Deliver

    Stills, film or an interactive study, handed over with the working files and the reasoning that made them.

06 Questions — before saying hello

Fig. 06

Three honest answers.

If the question you have is not here, ask it directly — the enquiry sheet in the footer is the way in.

Q.01 What should we send to begin? +

Three things help: a model if one exists, drawings or screenshots if it does not, and a few written lines about what the project is for. We reply with a reading of the project before any fee is discussed.

Q.02 Do you work from finished models only? +

No. Concept-stage work is often the most rewarding — the pavilion on this site began as a massing study. You can arrive with a sketch, a plan or only an argument, and the visual work will follow the argument.

Q.03 How does a quote and a start date work? +

Each study is scoped individually — imagery, film and interactive work carry different weights. Begin the conversation through the enquiry sheet in the footer; contact details are being supplied for the build and will appear there once confirmed.
